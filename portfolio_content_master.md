## 5. The Developer Journal (Notebook Entries)

### 5.1 Tab 1: On Craft

#### March 2025 — *Thoughts on intention vs decoration in UI*
> *"I've been thinking about why some apps feel alive and others feel like spreadsheets dressed up. It's not the animations — it's the intention. When you build something like you're composing music, the user feels it even if they can't name it. Every spacing decision, every transition, every colour choice is a word in a sentence. Say something worth saying."*

#### January 2025 — *Code as sculpting — Blender taught me subtraction*
> *"The best code I've ever written didn't feel like engineering. It felt like sculpting. I removed things until only the true shape remained. Blender taught me that. You start with too much and whittle it down. Functions, components, dependencies — the less that's there, the more powerful what remains becomes. Simplicity isn't laziness. It's the hardest thing to achieve."*

#### November 2024 — *Slipping soul into a 'corporate' client project*
> *"A client once told me 'make it look professional.' I asked what they meant. They said, 'you know — clean, corporate.' I built it clean, yes. But I slipped some soul in where they wouldn't notice. The micro-interactions. The hover states. The way the page breathes. They loved it. They didn't know why. That's the whole game — make it feel inevitable, like it couldn't have been built any other way."*

---

### 5.2 Tab 2: On Games & Art

#### February 2025 — *Death Stranding replay — connection as game mechanic*
> *"Finished Death Stranding again. I always come back to it when I need to remember why I do this. Kojima builds worlds that argue with you. The gameplay IS the philosophy — connection, loneliness, the weight of carrying something for someone else. Every strand you build makes the world slightly less hostile for a stranger you'll never meet. I want to make software that does that. Products that quietly make someone's day less difficult."*

#### October 2024 — *MGS2 predicted post-truth media in 2001*
> *"Gaming is unfairly dismissed as entertainment. Metal Gear Solid 2 predicted post-truth media manipulation in 2001 — AI-controlled information, the collapse of context, the manufacturing of reality. Death Stranding asks you to reconsider individualism through the act of delivering a parcel. What other medium does that? Every game I love is an argument. I build apps as arguments too. What does this product believe? What is it trying to prove?"*

#### August 2024 — *A 3D personal project no one will ever see*
> *"Started a 3D project just for myself. No client, no deadline, no brief. A small room. Low-poly. Warm light coming through a window that doesn't exist. A desk with objects that mean something only to me. It's the best thing I've made and no one will ever see it. That's probably why it's good. There's a version of every project that exists before the client gets involved — I'm trying to get my client work to feel like that version."*

---

### 5.3 Tab 3: On Work

#### April 2025 — *QC app goes live — watching the machinist use it*
> *"The quality-check app went live on the factory floor. I drove out to watch someone use it for the first time. A machinist — big hands, no patience for bad UI — picked up the phone, squinted at the screen, poked around for about twenty seconds, then nodded and got on with his job. Twenty seconds. No tutorial needed. No confusion. That's the whole point. Software should disappear into the work. When it does, that's the best feeling in this job."*

#### December 2024 — *How AI tools compress the distance between idea and artifact*
> *"AI tools have changed everything about how I work. Not because they write my code for me — they don't, not really. But because they compress the distance between idea and artifact. I can prototype in an hour what used to take a week. The creative energy goes somewhere better now. Instead of spending it on boilerplate, I spend it on decisions that actually matter — what does this product believe? What does it feel like to use? Those questions deserve the full budget."*

#### September 2024 — *The vegetable price app and quiet significance*
> *"Working on a vegetable price app for a local market sounds unglamorous. No VC funding. No launch post. No Hacker News thread. But every morning, someone in Puducherry checks their phone before heading out to buy tomatoes. My code is in that moment. It helps them decide whether to go to the market or the shop, whether to cook what they planned or switch the menu. That small, practical, quiet moment of usefulness — that's quietly remarkable. Not all impact is loud."*
